# Field report 2026-10-29: the first round

The first round of the field campaign (plan M13): twenty public sites that
run bot defences, each read at level 3 by one persona of the owner's pool,
so that how often each vendor turns Kage away, per engine and pacing, is
known for the first time. A session ran it on the owner's typed approval
of the list (2026-10-24), from 24 to 29 October 2026. No site is named
here: a site is its index, the country of the persona that read it, its
category and the vendor found. The mapping stays with the owner.

## What ran

- **Kage** `77f6978` (the code of 2.0.0; the images built locally from it,
  2.0.0 not yet tagged), as a compose project of its own on the owner's
  machine (`docs/runbooks/field-campaign.md`, "A session runs it").
- **Pool**: twenty static residential proxies, four each in AT, DE, FR, GB
  and IT; twenty new personas, four per country: 9 Chrome 155 claiming
  Linux, 8 Firefox 156 claiming Windows, 3 claiming macOS. Each country's
  sites were dealt to its personas by a seeded draw: 9 sites read by
  Chrome, 11 by Firefox. Every batch ran in a session pinned to its site's
  persona: no persona read two sites, and a block was never retried by
  another persona. Warm-up on (below, "Warm-up (F85)").
- **Every site's entry** (the key anonymised; site 5's second pass with
  `pacing: patient`, the only change of configuration in the round):

  ```yaml
    "site-N.example":
      min_level: 3
      countries: [<its persona's>]
      engines: [<its persona's>]
      pacing: human
      pacing_locked: true
      behavior_locked: true
      robots: enforce
      active_hours: enforce
      retries: 2
      breaker_threshold: 5
      breaker_pause: 30m
      referer_strategy: direct
      min_dwell: 2s
      driver_input: false
  ```

- **robots.txt** enforced, with `robots.agents: ["kage", "Claude-User"]`:
  the round was driven by Claude acting for the owner, so a group a site
  writes for `Claude-User` applied in place of its group for everybody
  (one site has one). Each site's scout (another persona, never its
  reader) asked for the file. The crawls were kept off 950 disallowed
  links; no page read was disallowed; no site sets a `Crawl-delay`.
- **A batch**: one crawl from the site's home page in the reader's order,
  no sitemaps, a deny list for sign-in, account, basket, checkout, search
  and form pages (and each site's own words for them), level 3,
  `include: ["headers", "extract"]`; from the first day's eleventh batch
  on, the documents were kept inside the stack for the consent check and
  removed with it.
- **Schedule.** Sunday 25 October: ten sites, 10 pages at depth 2, two at
  a time. From Tuesday 27 October, on the owner's order typed in the
  session: 40 pages at depth 3, five at a time and one per country, a
  second pass four hours or more after a block. The owner's machine was
  off from Sunday evening to Tuesday 16:56 UTC and from Tuesday evening to
  Thursday 17:20 UTC, so the slices of Monday and Wednesday did not run.
- **Bandwidth**: 851 MB through the pool in all, about 400 MB of it the
  warm-up and 456 MB the twenty sites (1.0 MB a page); 0.3 % of the plan's
  month. 442 pages asked of the sites by the round's key, of a budget of
  2,000.
- **Controls**: two pages with no defence on the first day, both read; the
  later slices ran no controls.

## Findings

- **F85, a warm-up visit is judged on no document.** Every warm-up visit
  was judged a soft block (108 made), because a warm-up visit asks the
  sidecar for no document and the classifier then sees an empty page. The
  visits were made as designed; their verdicts mean nothing (above). Fixed
  on `main` before the 2.0.0 tag.
- **F88, a page whose head outgrows a mebibyte was judged blank.** The
  signatures read the first `settings.max_body_bytes` (1 MiB) of a captured
  document; a page whose scripts had grown its head past that before the
  visit ended left the classifier nothing but head, and site 15's first
  batch was judged a blank page (set aside above). The shipped signatures
  now read 16 MiB (this pull request, with a test); the code's own answer,
  rules that count text abstaining on a body that was cut, is F88.
- **F90, a 304 to the scout was taken for an unreachable robots.txt.** A
  scout that asks a day later revalidates the copy in its browser's cache
  and gets 304 Not Modified, which Kage took for an unreachable file: under
  `enforce` nothing of the site could be read, an hour at a time. It set
  aside site 3's first try at its second pass and cut site 11's batch after
  38 pages (the http origin of the same host). The round gave three sites
  another scout; the fix is F90 (a 304 keeps the stored file).
- **A branded edge denial.** Site 18's edge denied its first page with a
  page of the site's own branding (no "Access Denied" text) and the edge's
  error reference in `x-reference-error`; Kage judged it a bare 403.
  `akamai-reference-error` now judges it akamai / access_denied (this pull
  request, with a synthetic fixture).
- **A declared client refused its robots.txt.** Site 11 answered its first
  scout's request for `/robots.txt` with a bare 403 and read nothing to it
  under `enforce`, which is the behaviour wanted; a second scout read the
  file and the site's pages two days later. Site 3 refused its new scout
  the file on its second pass and was left alone.
- **The warm-up is heavy.** About 400 MB of the round's 851 MB were
  warm-up visits; one country's weather service cost about 40 MB a visit.
  Together with F83 (`Special:Random` out of the shipped list), the
  warm-up list deserves a look at what each site costs.
- **The `patient` profile** (shorter bursts, about twice the gaps and
  rests, one visitor) is added to `policies/pacing-profiles.yaml` as the
  round's one pacing change: under it site 5 read 39 of 40 pages where
  `human` met an interstitial at page 10. One site and one day is thin
  evidence; it is a profile to choose, not a default.
