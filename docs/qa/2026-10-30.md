# QA round one, 2026-10-30

One QA agent per topic ran against the stack in the closed fixture world
(`deploy/compose.e2e.yaml`, the fixture site, and a programmable responder
that answers as whatever site a test needs), hunting for behaviour that
contradicts the specification, the OpenAPI document, the runbooks or plain
correctness. The session that ran them reproduced every finding below on a
stack of its own before it entered this report. No code was changed: a
confirmed bug is a finding, and fixes are separate pull requests on the
owner's word.

- **Commit under test:** `15ba60e` (2.0.1 and what followed, contract 1.6.0).
  All three images, `kage:dev`, `kage-browser-chromium:dev` and
  `kage-browser-firefox:dev`, were built from it.
- **Topics:** API contract, authentication and scopes, SSRF and egress, jobs
  and webhooks, the crawler, extraction and the cache, the classifier and
  the policies, scheduler and pacing, CLI and configuration, observability
  and secrecy, documents against behaviour.
- **Result:** 103 findings after merging duplicates (111 filed): 3 critical,
  14 high, 32 medium, 54 low. Each carries the agent's id, and a merged one
  both ids. Items already known are cited by F-number and not counted.

No site, key, address or token is in this document. `qa.kage.test` and
`other.qa.test` are names of the responder and `*.kage.test` of the fixture
site, which exist on the compose network only; a private or metadata
address is named by its kind. In the reproductions, `P` is a page of the
responder that the row describes and `U` any page of the fixture site.

## How to read a severity

| Severity | Meaning |
|---|---|
| critical | another key's data reaches someone who must not have it, a request reaches a private target, or a site can take the server down |
| high | a documented guarantee fails in normal use, or wrong results are returned as right |
| medium | a contract mismatch a client hits, with a way around it |
| low | a mismatch nobody is hurt by, a misleading message, a document out of date |

## What the round did not cover, and one breach of its rule

- Not exercised: level 1 (no persona has an http profile, F30); `https`
  targets (the world is plain http, so what depends on TLS, among it F85 by
  behaviour and F90's 304 path); a replacement that really replaces; real
  lengths of TTLs, retention and the webhook schedule (shortened config and

  closed container, panel queries read against the exposition instead); the
  /24 and ASN rules (all addresses of the world are one /24); `human` bursts
  and `patient` visit breaks to their full length.
- **Isolation.** A DROP rule on everything leaving the nested Docker was on
  from the first stack, and it dropped 33 SYNs. The first 27 fell before
  destinations were recorded; the SSRF agent's deliberate tests of names with
  no route explain them, as far as can be told afterwards: rerun, one such
  test moved the counter by the same kind of packet, and the one destination
  on record for all the later ones is that test's reserved address. Nothing
  dropped reached anyone.
- **A breach.** One agent ran the debug binary's `kage proxy health` in the
  outer container, outside that rule, with default URLs. That command asks
  the configured host-address lookup service, a public one, so one GET
  probably left the machine, with no credential and no site's data in it. It
  cannot be told afterwards whether it did. The outer container's own way out
  was closed the moment this was reported, and no agent has had a way out
  since. Every other request of the round stayed in the fixture world.
