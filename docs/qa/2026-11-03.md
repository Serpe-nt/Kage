# QA round two, 2026-11-03

Commit under test: `91b7ab2` (`main` on 2026-11-03, after rounds A and B
fixed F96 to F111 in #35 to #44). The three images (`kage`, both sidecars),
the CLI and the node projects were built from it in a development container
with a Docker daemon of its own; every stack was the closed fixture world of
the end-to-end tests (`deploy/compose.e2e.yaml`) plus the QA harness and the
worlds this round added (`tests/qa/`, in this pull request). Before the
first stack a DROP rule closed every way out of the nested Docker and of the
container itself (round one had closed only the first, and had one breach;
see "The firewall"). No request went to a real site or host, no real key or
proxy was used, `KAGE_LIVE` was never set.

The round had three parts, in this order of weight: the gaps round one could
not reach (its section "What the round did not cover"), a regression sweep of
round one's 111 reproductions, and an adversarial look at the edges of what
rounds A and B fixed. Six agents worked one topic each (`tls`, `pool`,
`ssrf`, `timers`, `http`, `flows`), three to six at a time, and a seventh
(`left`) took what they named as not reached; every finding below
was reproduced again by this session on a fresh stack, or on the stack of its
own its script brings up, before it entered this report, and its evidence log
read. What could not be reproduced was dropped; what is already known is
named by its F-number and not counted.

**Result:** 32 new findings: 9 high, 11 medium, 12 low, none critical. The 28
reproductions of F96 to F111 all end `NOT-REPRODUCED`; every open item of
round one still reproduces except where a fix took it in passing (two rows,
below). Seven of the high findings are places where a fix of rounds A and B
does not reach: F102 for schemes and for a Firefox meta refresh under
`direct` (ssrf-06, ssrf-02), F107 for a pool of four or more proxies
(pool-02), F105 for `--persona` and `kage persona selftest` (flows-04), F96
for a tab a flow's click opens (flows-02), F101 for `domain_concurrency` at a
flow's later site (left-01), and F108 for a proxy's refusal of one target in
Firefox (left-02); another is the one-address rule of D50 for a level 0
redirect to another site (http-02). One more case is known (G1, F57) but
worse than its rating says: see "Known, with a question".

## How to read a severity

| Severity | Meaning |
|---|---|
| critical | another key's data reaches someone who must not have it, a request reaches a private target, or data is lost |
| high | a documented guarantee fails in normal use, wrong results are returned as right, or a fix of rounds A and B does not hold |
| medium | a contract mismatch a client hits, with a way around it (or none, when the documents disagree with each other) |
| low | a mismatch nobody is hurt by, a misleading message, a document out of date |

The ids are the agents' (`<topic>-NN`); each has a `finding.md` and a
`repro.sh` ending `REPRODUCED` or `NOT-REPRODUCED` in the round's evidence
(outside the repository, as round one's). A repro that needs a world of its
own says so on its second line and brings it up from `tests/qa/infra/`.

## Known, with a question

- **ssrf-01, G1 and F57.** With `security.resolve_on_host` under the pool, a
  public page's 302, meta refresh, script redirect, form, or a flow's click or
  submit, sends the browser to a name that resolves to a private address; the
  forwarder judges the browser's request by URL, the request reaches the
  private target (the private responder logged both engines), and the private
  page comes back as `ok` at levels 2 and 3. The same name asked for directly
  is `422 ssrf_denied`. The security review's G1 names the first half ("a
  name that resolves into the operator's network passes the forwarder") at
  low, and F57's follow-up is the forwarder resolving names; the review also
  says "what the check prevents is that anything read there is returned or
  stored", which the main frame's redirect chain, checked by URL only, does
  not prevent here. By this round's scale that is critical: **should F57 be
  raised, and the after-the-fact check of a navigation's chain resolve names
  under the setting as the http path's hops do since F98?** Not counted.
- Met and still as the plan says: F30 (no persona has an http profile, so
  level 1 is `422 level_unavailable`), F90, F126 (the breaker gauge lost on a
  restart), F150 (the sidecars' counters born with their first event), F151
  (a dead pool of four or more stays `healthy`; pool-02 is the scheduler's
  side of it), F159 (a stopped proxy's name is slow to fail), F160, F162.

Each topic's record, from its agent's coverage file (in the evidence, one

**https (tls).** An https world: a private CA and certificates made at run

fixture site, the product's own `kage echo serve` over TLS, and every
persona's Chromium and Camoufox profile made to trust the CA by writing it

the host's normal form; private subresources and hops over CONNECT refused
under the pool and `direct`, nothing private reached a proxy; webhooks verify
the receiver's certificate.

## The firewall

A DROP on `DOCKER-USER -o eth0` (the nested Docker's containers) and on the
development container's own `OUTPUT -o eth0`, both from before the first
stack, NFLOG-captured from 21:53Z on; both rules set again, before any stack,
in the container made again after the pause. `DOCKER-USER` dropped 0 packets
over the whole round. The container's own `OUTPUT` dropped 8220 packets in
the first container and 0 in the second, plus one curl of mine to a
documentation address that proved the rule. They are DNS queries from the
container's Docker daemon to Docker Desktop's internal resolver, for names the
world's own resolver does not know, which Docker's embedded resolver forwards
upstream: the capture holds 2436 of them, every one DNS on port 53, and the
5784 before it began were of the same kind by a sample. Most are the names of
stopped proxy containers (F159); then gRPC's `_grpc_config.localhost` of the
observability images, a few of the agents' unknown test names, and eight
lookups of a search engine's own name, at one moment during this session's
reproduction of ssrf-03 and ssrf-05 on `direct` stacks: Chromium's background
requests, resolved by the forwarder under `direct`, as the levels-and-egress
runbook and the security review (T5) say they are. Nothing reached anyone.
