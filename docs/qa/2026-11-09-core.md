# QA round four, part A (the core), 2026-11-09

Commit under test: `f61fc9e` (`main` on 2026-11-09 after round F's core
work: #74, #76 to #78, #81, #83, #84, #86, #87 and #89). The three images
(`kage`, both sidecars) were built from it in a development container
(`kage-qa4-core`) with a Docker daemon of its own; the CLI is the `kage`
image's own binary, run with no network (`tests/qa/cli/k4.sh`), so no
separate cargo build was made. Every stack was the closed fixture world of
the end-to-end tests (`deploy/compose.e2e.yaml`) plus the QA harness and its
worlds (`tests/qa/`; this pull request adds round four's). Before the first
stack a DROP rule, with every dropped packet copied to an NFLOG capture,
closed every way out of the nested Docker and of the container itself; it
stayed on until the end. No request went to a real site or host, no real
key or proxy was used, `KAGE_LIVE` was never set, and the F220 workflow was
read, never triggered.

Round four has two parts. This one, the core, covers the scheduler and
jobs, the single-server lock, the API, the CLI and the configuration, the
SSRF guard and the forwarder, the pool and level 0, and observability. Part
B (another session, its own container and report) covers the browsers, the
sidecar, typing, extraction, the crawl and the classifier, and sweeps their
earlier repros. The weight here is on what rounds D, E and F changed: does a
fix hold, does it reach the road next to the one it was made for, did it
break something beside it. Six agents worked one topic each (`sched`,
`lock`, `api`, `guard`, `pool0`, `obs`), at most three at a time. This
session ran the regression sweep of rounds one to three in its area, and
reproduced every finding below again, on a fresh stack or on the stack of
its own its script brings up, before it entered this report. Nothing was
dropped for failing to reproduce. What is already known is named by its
F-number and not counted.

**Result:** 25 new findings: 6 high, 4 medium, 15 low, none critical. The
regression sweep ran 124 of rounds one to three's reproductions in this
area: no fixed item reproduces again (below). Two of round three's could
not run for want of the 2.0.2 release images (ops-02, ops-03); one of round
three's, dropped then because that session could not reproduce it, now
reproduces and is filed (lock-05).

Five of the six high findings are a fix of round E or F that does not reach
the road next to its own:

- a flow's timeout behind a redirect is booked to the flow's first site or
  to the redirecting one, never to the silent site (F162 and flows-04 miss
  redirects; sched-01);
- a server that stops for an unconfirmed lock (lock-02's fix) writes the
  pages it was reading `failed` / `internal` once the network heals, also
  after another server took the lock and re-read them (lock-01);
- a proxy's `502` to a tunnel is the proxy's, retried on other personas and
  reported to the pool, where §8.3 (F108, F189) says it is the target's
  (guard-03);
- `robots.allowed: true` beside a `robots_unavailable` refusal when nothing
  could be had at all, and at the end of a flow on a site whose file is
  kept unreachable (level0-04's fix; pool0-01, pool0-05).

The sixth is a leak of identity the documents rule out: a session opened
for a site can be pinned to the site's robots scout, so the address that
sent the site `kage/<version>` at level 0 then reads it as a browser
(pool0-04). At the time of writing the planner had routed every high
finding: pool0-01 and pool0-05 to #91, guard-03 to #92, pool0-04 to a pull
request of its own, lock-01 and sched-01 to the next fix session.

## How to read a severity

| Severity | Meaning |
|---|---|
| critical | another key's data reaches someone who must not have it, a request reaches a private target, or data is lost |
| high | a documented guarantee fails in normal use, wrong results are returned as right, or a fix of rounds D, E and F does not hold |
| medium | a contract mismatch a client hits, with a way around it (or none, when the documents disagree with each other) |
| low | a mismatch nobody is hurt by, a misleading message, a document out of date |

"High by the round's rule" marks a finding that is high because a fix of
rounds D to F does not hold on a road next to its own, while its harm alone
would be lower; pool0-01 and pool0-05 are such, and the planner rates
guard-03 medium-high. The ids are the agents' (`<topic>-NN`); each has a
`finding.md` and a `repro.sh` ending `REPRODUCED` or `NOT-REPRODUCED` in the
round's evidence (outside the repository, as the earlier rounds'). A repro
that needs a world or a configuration of its own says so on its second line
and brings it up from `tests/qa/infra/`; the rest run on a stack after
`stack.sh up` and `stack.sh fast`.
