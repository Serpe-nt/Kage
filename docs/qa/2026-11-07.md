# QA round three, 2026-11-07

Commit under test: `5cbcc9f` (`main` on 2026-11-07: 2.0.4 with round D's
scheduler fixes #65, extraction fixes #66, the release workflow #70 and the
test fix F215). The three images (`kage`, both sidecars), the CLI and the
node projects were built from it in a development container (`kage-qa3`)
with a Docker daemon of its own; the typing topic ran on sidecar images
rebuilt from `9f8eb14` once #69 (F202) merged (nothing under `crates/`
changed between the two). Every stack was the closed fixture world of the
end-to-end tests (`deploy/compose.e2e.yaml`) plus the QA harness and its
worlds (`tests/qa/`, this pull request adds round three's). Before the first
stack a DROP rule, with every dropped packet copied to an NFLOG capture,
closed every way out of the nested Docker and of the container itself. No
request went to a real site or host, no real key or proxy was used,
`KAGE_LIVE` was never set. The release chain was checked read-only from the
host against the project's own registry and GitHub release.

The round had four parts: what rounds C and D changed, with the most weight
on the single-server lock (F174), flows and cancels (F175, F128, F130, F156),
extraction (F122, F147, F173, F141, F142) and the browsers (F178, F190,
F200, F201, F204, F202); the upgrade path and the first start; the release
chain; and what the earlier rounds could not reach and round C's F191 made
reachable (level 0 over `https`, Kage's own outbound clients under a private
CA). Seven agents worked one topic each (`lock`, `flows`, `extract`,
`browser`, `ops`, `level0`, `api`), five to seven at a time, and an eighth
(`bgcalls`) took the planner's question on the browsers' own calls to
Google that d-extract's gate captured under `direct`; this session
ran the regression sweep and the release check, and reproduced every finding
below again on a fresh stack, or on the stack of its own its script brings
up, before it entered this report. What could not be reproduced was dropped
(one: lock-03). What is already known is named by its F-number and not
counted.

**Not in this report: the crawl and the classifier** (F116, F118 to F120,
F125, F143). Their fixes (#72) had not merged when the report was finished;
that topic is tested after #72, in an addendum (a second, small pull
request). Their round one reproductions still reproduce on `5cbcc9f`, as
expected (the regression sweep).

**Result:** 41 new findings: 5 high, 18 medium, 18 low, none critical. The
regression sweep ran all 111 of round one's and all 33 of round two's
reproductions as filed: no fixed item reproduces again; every row that
reproduces is an open item or a fix made in the documents only (below).
Four of the high findings are places where a fix of round C or D does not
hold on a road next to the one it was made for: a flow's cancel (F175)
frees the persona while the sidecar still works on it (flows-01); a job
waiting out a backoff is still woken every minute (F156, flows-02); a
proxy's refusal of a level 0 redirect hop is the site's (F189, level0-01);
F202's typing loses the second of two characters on one key (browser-06).
The fifth is the hang F174 was opened for, on the one-server road it kept:
after a killed server is started again, a resumed crawl page meets the dead
server's operation and stays `running` for good (lock-01). flows-01 and
lock-01 share a root: the sidecar's `context_busy` from an operation Kage no
longer waits for. The release chain of 2.0.4 verifies end to end. The 37
SYNs to Google that d-extract's gate dropped are the host persona's Chromium
at `direct.sh`'s level 3 fetch (its engine is a random draw, hence "timing");
of what Chrome asks Google by itself, one call is not idle and named nowhere:
Google's Autofill server is asked about every page with a form a persona
reads (bgcalls-01).

## How to read a severity

| Severity | Meaning |
|---|---|
| critical | another key's data reaches someone who must not have it, a request reaches a private target, or data is lost |
| high | a documented guarantee fails in normal use, wrong results are returned as right, or a fix of rounds C and D does not hold |
| medium | a contract mismatch a client hits, with a way around it (or none, when the documents disagree with each other) |
| low | a mismatch nobody is hurt by, a misleading message, a document out of date |

The ids are the agents' (`<topic>-NN`); each has a `finding.md` and a
`repro.sh` ending `REPRODUCED` or `NOT-REPRODUCED` in the round's evidence
(outside the repository, as the earlier rounds'). A repro that needs a world
of its own says so on its second line and brings it up from `tests/qa/infra/`
(api-09's adds a server beside a stack that is up, so it runs on one).
